Learn Skiff

The manual for Skiff, the six-track groovebox firmware for the M-VAVE FM-1, in 19 chapters. Each chapter has a short video that shows every press, hold and turn on the unit, made from the firmware itself. Play it and the steps below follow along. New to grooveboxes? Start at chapter 1 and read on. Know your way around? Jump to any chapter.

START HERE

Install Skiff

You'll need an FM-1, a USB data cable, and Chrome or Edge on a computer. You'll learn how to put Skiff on the unit, update it, and go back to the official firmware.

  • The Skiff installer runs in the browser: connect the FM-1 by USB and follow it. It takes about two minutes and the FM-1 restarts on Skiff.
  • No Chrome or Edge? With Python 3, tools/fm1_install.py from the repository installs the release's .fwsc file.
  • Later releases install the same way. Holding OCT− and OCT+ together for five seconds puts the unit in update mode.
  • The same page takes the FM-1 back to the official V15 firmware, saving a complete backup of your music first.

Find your way around

You'll need Skiff on the unit. You'll learn the pages, the four knobs, the quick layers, SHIFT and the MENU: how every other chapter gets around.

  1. HOME

    HOME is the page with all six tracks, a lane each with its steps and the playhead: the selected track's lane large, its sound's name over its engine, the other five compact. The header shows the bar, the beat and the tempo.

    Here the beat you make in chapter 3 is already in, so there's something to hear: tap PLAY to start it, and again to stop it.

  2. Pick a track

    Turn ALGORITHM: it picks the track, T1–T6, on every page. On HOME the selected track's lane grows and takes its colour. The keys always play the selected track: here track 2, PAD, then back to track 1, ACID.

  3. Four knobs, four cards

    KNOB 1–KNOB 4, top right, turn the four cards across the top of the screen, in the same order. Every page has its own four. On HOME they're the selected track's main sound controls, for ACID CUTOFF, RES, ATTACK and RELEASE: here KNOB 1 opens the filter and closes it again.

  4. Change the sound

    PRESETS changes the selected track's sound, a detent a sound, through every engine's: ACID, SINE KEY, RAVE. Two detents back and it's ACID again. On the step pages PRESETS moves the cursor instead; chapter 4 finds sounds by type.

  5. Pages

    The twelve buttons under the knobs open pages, each a family of them. Tap ENV for the envelope, again for ENV DEST (where the envelope goes), again for ENV 2. The footer counts them, 1/3 to 3/3, and the lit button is the family you're in.

  6. Turn the pages

    SELECT turns the family's pages too, one a detent, either way, stopping at the first and the last. On every page the four knobs edit its cards: here ENV DEST's KNOB 1, CUTOFF, lets the envelope open ACID's filter all the way.

  7. Where you left it

    SEQ opens its family on the first page, STEP: the bass line's 16 steps. Tap ENV and it opens on ENV DEST, the page you left; every family does that. HOME goes back to HOME from anywhere.

    The first time you tap a button, the screen says HOLD and its name: SHORTCUTS. That's the next step.

  8. Hold for a layer

    Hold FX, GLO, SEL, EDIT, SEQ or ARP and its layer covers the page: a map of what the keys and the four knobs do while you hold it. The keys you press then are the layer's, not notes. A quick tap still opens the pages.

    GLO's layer is the mix: the white keys from F3 solo tracks 1–6 while held, the black keys mute them (F♯3 track 1 to F♯4 track 6), and the knobs are four tracks' levels. Here C♯4 mutes the drums and brings them back. Let go and the page is back.

  9. Keep it open

    Hold HOME (that's SHIFT) and tap the layer's button: the layer stays open with nothing held, and the header says GLO LOCKED. Here F♯3 mutes the bass and brings it back. Tap GLO again to close it (HOME closes it too). Holding the button and then pressing HOME does the same.

  10. SHIFT

    Hold HOME on its own. After a moment a map covers the page, with what every control does on it. On HOME: PRESETS the sound, GLO + SELECT the tempo, ALGORITHM the track, a held button its layer, SAVE held undo, and the SHIFT combos, such as SHIFT + SAVE for TAKE. Every page has its own map, and its footer says LET GO: MENU.

  11. The MENU

    Let go of HOME without touching anything else, and the MENU opens: the unit's settings, in five tabs, DISPLAY, CONTROL, AUDIO, SYSTEM and MIDI. ALGORITHM changes the tab, PRESETS the row, and any of KNOB 1–KNOB 4, or OCT− and OCT+, its value. The music plays on meanwhile. HOME closes it. Chapter 18 goes through the settings.

  12. Yes or back

    Wherever the unit asks or acts, OCT+ does it and OCT− goes back; the footer says what each one does there. Tap SAVE, turn SELECT to PROJECT, and KNOB 3 picks NEW. OCT+ would start a new project, so a dialog asks first: NEW PROJECT? The music now goes, and it can't be undone.

  13. Cancel

    CANCEL is the dialog's default: OCT−, and nothing has changed. HOME goes back to HOME, from any page.

Your first beat

You'll need Skiff on the unit (chapter 1). You'll learn the drum grid, PLAY and the tempo, writing a bass line step by step, changing a sound and undo: a beat in two minutes.

  1. A new unit

    Start on HOME, the page with all six tracks. Fresh from the installer, each track has a sound and no notes: 1 ACID, 2 PAD, 3 PULSE LD, 4 DRUM KIT, 5 PIANO and 6 DRUM KIT. Play a few white keys: the keys play the selected track, here 1, ACID.

    Made music already? Start a new project instead: tap SAVE and turn SELECT to PROJECT, pick an empty slot with KNOB 1, turn KNOB 3 to NEW and press OCT+. Your other projects stay saved.

  2. Pick the drums

    Turn ALGORITHM: it picks the track, on every page. Turn it three detents to track 4, DRUM KIT. Now the keys play the drums: C4 is the kick, D4 the snare, F♯4 the closed hat.

  3. Open the grid

    Tap SEQ. On a drum track its STEP page is a grid: a row for each drum (BD bass drum, SD snare, CP clap, CH closed hat, OH open hat, TM tom, RS rim, CB bell) and a column for each step. The 16 white keys are the 16 steps, left to right. You don't need REC for this.

    A black key picks a row, and plays its drum: F♯3 the kick, G♯3 the snare, C♯4 the closed hat.

  4. Play

    Tap PLAY. It lights green, and a light runs along the white keys: the playhead. The keys on the beats (1, 5, 9, 13) glow a little brighter, to count by.

  5. Kick

    F♯3 picks the kick row again. Now tap the white keys for steps 1, 5, 9 and 13: F3 C4 G4 D5. A kick on every beat.

  6. Snare

    G♯3 picks the snare row. Steps 5 and 13, C4 and D5: the backbeat.

  7. Hats

    C♯4 picks the closed hat. Steps 3, 7, 11 and 15, between the beats: A3 E4 B4 F5.

    A key on a step that already has a hit takes it away: tap F5 again and the last hat goes, once more and it's back.

  8. Tempo

    Hold GLO and turn SELECT: a detent is one BPM, and the header shows the tempo. Here it goes from 120 to 110. (SELECT alone turns pages; MENU › CONTROL › BPM LOCK OFF makes it the tempo knob, as on Felucca.)

  9. A bass line

    Tap PLAY to stop, then turn ALGORITHM back to track 1. You stay on SEQ › STEP, which now shows track 1's 16 steps as tiles, the cursor on step 1.

  10. Write steps

    Tap OCT− once, so the keys play an octave lower. Then tap REC: stopped, on the STEP page, the screen says KEYS WRITE STEPS. Each key you play is written to the step under the cursor, the white tile, and the cursor moves on. PRESETS moves the cursor too, so one detent leaves a step empty.

    The line here: A, rest, A, rest, A an octave up, rest, A, rest, G, rest, A, rest, C, rest, E.

  11. Listen

    Tap REC again to stop writing, then PLAY: drums and bass together. (Armed while playing, the keys would record live instead, at the playhead: that's chapter 7.)

  12. Change the sound

    Tap HOME and turn PRESETS: track 1 plays the same notes with each sound you reach, ACID, SINE KEY, RAVE, SUB BASS. On the step pages PRESETS moves the cursor instead; the sound changes on HOME and the sound pages. Chapter 4 finds sounds by type.

  13. Undo

    Hold SAVE: the last change is undone. A run of PRESETS turns counts as one change, so it's back to ACID. While the UNDO box shows, OCT− goes further back and OCT+ redoes. Eight changes are kept.

  14. Nothing to save

    Patterns have no save step: what a track plays is kept as its pattern, written to flash at the quiet autosave. Switch off and on, and with MENU › SYSTEM › RESTORE LAST on (it is, out of the box) the beat is still there.

04

MAKE MUSIC

Sounds and engines

You'll need chapter 3. You'll learn to find a sound by type, keep favourites, switch engines and save a sound of your own.

  1. A sound on every track

    Every track has a sound, its name on HOME over its engine's. PRESETS changes the selected track's, a sound a detent, through every engine's. Here the beat from chapter 3 plays with two chords on track 2: ALGORITHM to track 2, and PRESETS takes them from PAD to FM MARIMBA and FM ORGAN, then back.

  2. The list

    Back on track 1, tap SAVE and turn SELECT back to PRESETS. (The first time, SAVE opens USER; after that, the page you left.) Every sound is in the list, the selected track's marked, with its place (No. 5 of 148). KNOB 1 or PRESETS steps through it, and each sound plays as you reach it. The footer names the track's engine and sound.

  3. By type

    KNOB 4 is LIST: ALL, FAV, then the types, BASS, LEAD, PAD, KEYS, PLUCK, DRUM and FX. On BASS the list holds the basses of every engine, 19 of them, and PRESETS steps through those only: SUB BASS, NIGHT BASS, REESE.

  4. Any engine

    The list mixes the engines, and its left column says whose each sound is. FM BASS is FM6's: reach it and the track switches engine. Its notes, its mix and its effects stay as they were.

  5. Favourites

    KNOB 3, FAV: right marks the sound playing as a favourite, a star in the list; left unmarks it. LIST on FAV (KNOB 4 back one) shows your favourites only. Your own sounds, from SAVE › USER, are on ALL and FAV too.

  6. Engine to engine

    KNOB 2, ENG, jumps to the next engine's first sound, whatever the LIST: PHASE's PD BRASS, then LOFI's PULSE LD. With LIST still on FAV, PRESETS goes straight back to the favourite, FM BASS.

    The LIST stays where you leave it, through a power-off too, and it's the unit's, not the track's: on HOME and the sound pages PRESETS steps through it as well.

  7. Pick an engine

    Hold EDIT for its layer: the 14 engines on the white keys, F3 ANALOG to E5 DRUM-X, and on the knobs ENG, No. (the engine's sounds) and FAV. A key loads that engine's first sound on the selected track: here track 2, G4 GRAIN (CLOUD PAD), D4 VOICE (CHOIR AAH), F3 ANALOG (SAW LEAD). OCT−, with EDIT held, puts back the sound the track had. On tracks 5 and 6 PHYS and GRAIN are dark: they're for tracks 1–4.

  8. Make it yours

    On HOME the four knobs are the sound's main controls, on ANALOG CUTOFF, RES, ATTACK and RELEASE. Here KNOB 1 darkens SAW LEAD, KNOB 2 adds resonance and KNOB 4 lengthens the release, so the chords ring into each other. Every engine has more on its own pages: chapter 10.

  9. Start over

    In the EDIT layer the key after the engines, F5, is INIT: the sound starts over from its engine's first one, with everything else back to the engine's defaults (on FM6, a bare sine: chapter 11). It asks first, INITIALIZE SOUND?, and SAVE held would undo it. Here OCT− cancels: the sound stays as you made it.

  10. Save it

    Sounds of your own live in SAVE › USER, 32 slots. Saving writes to flash, so stop first: PLAY. Stopped, the keys still play the sound. Tap SAVE and turn SELECT to USER; KNOB 1 picks the slot, and SAVE is already picked: the footer says OCT+: SAVE.

  11. Name it

    OCT+ opens NAME with the slot's automatic name. The white keys type, phone style: AB on F3, CD on G3 and so on, the same key again within a moment for the next letter; another key moves on. C♯4 deletes (held, it repeats), G♯3 is a space, F♯3 and A♯3 move the cursor, D♯4 switches to digits; the black keys do the same in the upper octave.

    Here C♯4 held clears ANALOG 01, then WARM: B4 twice, F3, G4, D4 twice.

  12. Yours

    OCT+ writes it: SAVED U01, and U01 is WARM. PLAY, and track 2 plays on with it. Your sound is on the PRESETS lists ALL and FAV, and on SAVE › USER KNOB 2, LOAD, and OCT+ put it on whichever track is selected; KNOB 3 erases it, and one detent further renames it.

05

MAKE MUSIC

Keys, scales and the arp

You'll learn to play the keys in a scale, play chords from one key, and run the arpeggiator.

  1. The keys

    The keys play the selected track: 27 of them, F3 to G5. Here the beat from chapter 3 plays, and ALGORITHM picks track 5, PIANO, for this chapter.

  2. Octaves

    OCT+ moves the keys an octave up and OCT− an octave down, up to three each way; the header says OCT +1. Both together: back to the middle. Here one OCT− for the rest of the chapter.

  3. A scale

    Tap SEL for SCALE: ROOT, SCALE, QUANTIZE and TRANSP. KNOB 2 picks one of 16 scales, from CHR (all twelve notes) and MAJ to the modes, the pentatonics, BLUES and the diminished ones; MIN is the natural minor. The keyboard on the screen marks the scale's notes. On its own a scale changes nothing yet: QUANTIZE and the chord keys use it.

  4. The root

    Hold SEL for its layer and tap a key: its note name becomes the ROOT, whatever the octave. A3 makes it A minor, the bass line's key. The layer's knobs are ROOT, SCALE, CHORD and VOICING, its map shows the 16 scales, and OCT− puts back what you changed.

  5. In key

    KNOB 3, QUANTIZE. WHITE puts the scale on the white keys only: C4 plays the root, each white key the next note of the scale, up and down, and the black keys are silent, so every note is in key. SNAP keeps every key and rounds it down to the scale; SEQ also bends the sequencer's notes to the scale as they play, and SEQ+T transposes them by TRANSP first. KNOB 4, TRANSP, moves the keys by semitones.

  6. Chords

    Tap SEL again for CHORD. KNOB 1 picks the chord keys: DIA3, the scale's own triads, so each key plays the scale's chord on its note (here C4 A minor, A4 F, B4 G); DIA7 its sevenths; or one fixed shape on every key, MAJ, MIN, DOM7, MAJ7, MIN7, SUS4 or POW. The screen names the chord played last. MONO tracks play its root only.

  7. Voicing

    KNOB 2, VOICING, stacks the same notes another way: CLOSE, OPEN (the middle note an octave up), the inversions INV1 and INV2, and +OCT, the root added an octave down.

  8. The arp

    Tap ARP for the arpeggiator: MODE, RATE, OCTAVE and GATE. Turn KNOB 1, MODE, from OFF to UP (there are 14 modes) and hold a key: its notes play one after another at the RATE, in time with the beat. With chords on, one key is a whole arpeggio.

  9. Hold

    Tap ARP again for ARP 2: SWING, CHANCE, HOLD and ORDER. KNOB 3, HOLD ON, latches: let go and the arp plays on, and the next key or chord you play takes over (here F, then A minor again). ORDER plays the notes by pitch (NOTE) or as you played them (PLAYED).

  10. Range and length

    Turn SELECT back to ARP. KNOB 3, OCTAVE 2, runs the pattern over two octaves (up to four); KNOB 4, GATE, shortens each note. KNOB 2, RATE, sets the speed, 4 bars to 1/32 with the triplets. The latched arp plays on while you turn.

  11. Swing and chance

    SELECT to ARP 2 once more: KNOB 1, SWING, delays every other note, here by 30 %, and KNOB 2, CHANCE, lets each note play or rest by chance. REC records the arp as it does the keys: chapter 7.

06

MAKE MUSIC

Steps

You'll learn the step pages in full: notes and velocity on STEP, the piano roll, and a pattern's length, division and swing.

  1. The step page

    Tap SEQ for its first page, STEP: the selected track's bar as 16 tiles, two rows of eight like the halves of the keyboard, each note on its tile, the playing step's number lit. The white tile is the cursor: KNOB 1 moves it, and so does PRESETS on the step pages (the footer says PRESETS: STEP). Here the bass line from chapter 3 plays on track 1.

  2. Note

    KNOB 2, NOTE, moves the cursor step's note a semitone a detent: step 5's A3 down to G3. On an empty step the first detent puts in the last note used and the next ones move it: step 6 gets G3, then A3. No REC needed: the knobs edit the steps as they play.

  3. Velocity

    KNOB 3, VELOCITY: how hard the cursor step plays, 1 to 127. Step 7 goes down to 50, a little softer. A note put in with KNOB 2 shows 0: it plays at the default, 96, until you turn it.

  4. Chance

    KNOB 4, CHANCE: the chance the step plays each time round, 0 to 100 %. At 50 % the new note on step 6 comes and goes, and its tile gets a block in the corner. SEQ › CHANCE adds ratchets, flams and conditions: chapter 8.

  5. The piano roll

    Tap SEQ again for ROLL: the same steps on a piano roll, higher notes higher, the cursor's step framed. Its knobs are STEP, NOTE, TIME and FLAG.

  6. Tie

    KNOB 3, TIME: NOTE, TIE or REST. An empty step is a REST; one detent back makes it a TIE, so the note before it, step 13's C3, lasts through it.

  7. Slide

    KNOB 4, FLAG: ACC accents the step (it plays at full velocity), SLD slides it into the next note, A+S does both. Step 5's G3 now glides up into step 6's A3, the acid way.

  8. Length

    Turn SELECT to PATTERN, the pattern's own settings: LENGTH, DIV, SWING and GATE. KNOB 1, LENGTH, runs from 1 to 64 steps: at 8 the first half bar plays twice, back at 16 the whole line again. Past 16, the step pages turn with the playhead. DIV is a step's length, from 4 bars to 1/32 with the triplets; SWING delays every other step.

  9. Gate

    KNOB 4, GATE: how long every note of the pattern lasts, as a share of its step. Down to 27 % the line is short and tight. SEQ › PLAY gives a single step a GATE of its own.

  10. Clear a step

    Turn SELECT back to STEP. With REC armed, EDIT clears the cursor step and moves on: STEP CLEARED. Tap REC again to disarm it. (Armed while playing, the keys would record live; armed while stopped on STEP, they write steps: chapter 3.) SAVE held puts a cleared step back.

  11. Step preview

    STEP PREVIEW is a setting: hold HOME and let go for the MENU, ALGORITHM to CONTROL, PRESETS down to STEP PREVIEW, KNOB 1 to ON. It works when the music is stopped, so PLAY stops it, and HOME closes the MENU.

  12. Hear each step

    Stopped, with STEP PREVIEW on, every move of the cursor by KNOB 1 or PRESETS plays the step it lands on, once, on STEP, ROLL and the grid: here steps 8 to 15, the empty ones silent.

  13. Drum steps

    PLAY, and ALGORITHM to track 4: on a drum track STEP is the grid (chapter 3), and its knobs are STEP, LANE, HIT and ACC. KNOB 1 to step 13, KNOB 2 to the SNARE lane, KNOB 4 accents its hit (it brightens); KNOB 1 on to step 16 and KNOB 3 adds a snare there.

07

MAKE MUSIC

Recording and takes

You'll learn to record live with the click and a count-in, keep the loop you just played, and undo.

  1. Pick a track

    The beat from chapter 3 plays: tap PLAY, and turn ALGORITHM to track 5, PIANO. The keys play the selected track as always, and nothing is kept: track 5 has no notes, so its lane on HOME stays empty.

  2. REC

    Tap REC: the selected track is armed the moment it's pressed, and the header's play mark becomes a red dot. Armed and playing, the keys record at the playhead: a note goes into the step playing, or into the next one when it's played past the step's middle. Here a riff of eighths, E5 D5 C5 A4 and back up, which plays back on the next pass. A key held on ties into the steps after it. Stopped, REC starts the transport too; on a melodic STEP or ROLL page it writes steps instead (chapter 3).

  3. Overdub

    Still armed, every pass records on top of the last: what's there stays. Here a third under four of the notes. On a POLY sound like PIANO they join the step's notes as a chord, up to four; a MONO track's step takes the new note instead.

  4. Done

    Tap REC again to disarm. The riff plays on as it was recorded, kept as the track's pattern: no save needed.

  5. One pass, one undo

    Everything recorded between arming and disarming is one undo. Hold SAVE: UNDO RECORDING, the riff and its overdub gone together. While the box shows, OCT+ redoes it, and OCT− would go further back.

  6. The REC layer

    Hold HOME (SHIFT) and tap REC: the REC layer opens locked, REC LOCKED in the header. Its white keys are CLEAR, CLICK, COUNT-IN and CLICK LVL, each press the next value; its knobs are CLICK, COUNT-IN, CLICK LVL and FEEL. While it's open the keys are the layer's, so nothing plays or records, and OCT− puts back everything changed since it opened.

  7. Click

    KNOB 1, CLICK: OFF, REC (a click on every beat while a track is armed) or ON (on every beat while playing). The bar's first beat is higher. CLICK LVL sets how loud. The click goes to the headphones and the speaker only, never into USB audio; these are the MENU's AUDIO settings too.

  8. Count-in

    KNOB 2, COUNT-IN: OFF, 1 BAR or 2 BARS. With a track armed, starting from stop counts that many bars of clicks in first, whatever CLICK says. A note played in the last half beat of the count-in lands on step 1.

  9. Feel

    KNOB 4, FEEL ON: a note recorded into an empty step keeps where you played it, as the step's NUDGE, instead of landing square on the step (NUDGE: chapter 8). A take keeps it too. A step that already has notes keeps its own timing.

  10. Clear

    Hold F3, CLEAR: the header says HOLD: CLEAR, and after the hold time track 5's steps, all 64 of them, and its automation are gone: SEQUENCE CLEARED, T5 EMPTY. It's one undo, so SAVE held brings them back. The SEQ TOOLS clear too (chapter 8).

  11. Count in

    Tap REC to close the layer; that tap doesn't arm. PLAY stops, and REC arms the track: instead of starting at once, the screen counts the bar in, 4 3 2 1, a click on each beat, and then the music and the recording start together on step 1. PLAY during the count-in cancels it.

  12. As played

    The riff again, a little behind the beat: with FEEL on, each note keeps its lateness. REC disarms, and SEQ twice opens ROLL, the piano roll: every note sits a third of a step after its line, where it was played. With FEEL OFF they would sit on the lines.

  13. Take

    A TAKE keeps what you just played, without REC. Tap HOME, turn ALGORITHM to track 2, PAD, and play: here two chords, A minor and G, held through a bar. In the next bar hold HOME and tap SAVE: TAKE KEPT, 6 notes. The last whole pass of the track's loop goes into its steps, held notes as ties and knob moves as automation. It needs one whole pass played since PLAY, and REC off on that track.

  14. Undo

    Hold SAVE: UNDO TAKE, 1 of 4 undone, and the chords are gone. While the box shows, OCT− goes further back (UNDO RECORDING: track 5's riff goes too), and OCT+ forward again, twice, to where you were. Eight undos are kept, across all six tracks.

08

MAKE MUSIC

Variation

You'll learn to make a pattern move: chance, conditions, ratchets and flams, nudges, direction, locks, automation and the SEQ tools.

  1. The CHANCE page

    The beat from chapter 3: tap PLAY, turn ALGORITHM to track 4, tap SEQ for the grid and turn SELECT to CHANCE. Its knobs are STEP, CHANCE (0 to 100 %, the chance the step plays each time round), RATCHET and COND, for the step under the cursor; on a drum track they're the whole step's, every lane on it. KNOB 1 moves the cursor to step 15.

  2. Ratchet

    KNOB 3, RATCHET: X1 to X4 plays the step that many times inside its length. Step 15's hat at X3 is a quick roll into the next bar. The grid draws the hits split.

  3. Flam

    Past X4, RATCHET is FLAM: a softer grace hit, then the full one 15 ms later. KNOB 1 back to step 13 and KNOB 3 to FLAM: the kick and the snare there flam.

  4. Conditions

    KNOB 4, COND: when the step plays at all. FILL plays it only during a fill, !FILL only when there's none; A:B plays it in pass A of every B, counted from PLAY (1:2, 2:2, 1:3 … 4:4). Step 9's kick at 1:2 plays in the first bar of every two.

  5. A fill

    SELECT back to the grid, G♯3 picks the SNARE lane and G5 puts a snare on step 16 (chapter 3). SELECT on to CHANCE, where the cursor is now on 16: RATCHET X4 and COND FILL. The roll waits for a fill.

  6. Play the fill

    Hold GLO for its layer and hold F4, FILL: while it's held, every FILL step plays (and every !FILL step rests). Here the snare roll ends each bar for two bars, then the beat goes back to normal.

  7. A lock

    A lock is a step's own value for a knob. SELECT back to the grid, hold D5 (step 13) and turn KNOB 1: the cards show the sound's knobs, here DRUM's PITCH, TONE, DECAY and SNAP, and PITCH goes up on step 13 only. A mark over the step shows the lock. The knobs lock what they last edited: HOME's four after HOME, an EDIT, ENV, LFO or FX page's after that page. On a melodic track, hold the step's key while REC writes steps, stopped (chapter 3); EDIT tapped with the step held clears its locks.

  8. Direction

    ALGORITHM back to track 1 and SELECT to PLAY: STEP, NUDGE, DIR and GATE, with the piano roll. KNOB 3, DIR, is the track's: FWD, RVRS (backwards), PING (up and down) and RAND (any step each time). The bass line plays each in turn, then FWD again.

  9. Nudge

    KNOB 1 to step 7 and KNOB 2, NUDGE: the step plays early or late, −32 to +31 sixty-fourths of a step. At +16 it's a quarter of a step late, and the roll draws it where it plays. FEEL sets nudges as you record (chapter 7).

  10. A step's gate

    KNOB 1 to step 13 and KNOB 4, GATE: the step's own note length, a lock. It starts from the track's GATE, dimmed; turned, it's the step's (here 82 %), and turned back to the track's value it follows the track again.

  11. Automation

    Tap HOME, REC to arm track 1 while it plays, and turn KNOB 1, CUTOFF, up and down: each move is recorded on the step it's made in. REC again, and the bar plays the moves back: the card's value follows, a MOTION mark beside its label. HOME's knobs, the sound pages, the MIXER's LEVEL, PAN and REV record this way; at each loop's start the sound goes back to its own value.

  12. The list

    Tap SEQ (it reopens PLAY) and turn SELECT to AUTOMATION: every move and lock of the track, by step, AUTO or LOCK, the parameter and its value. KNOB 1 picks a row, KNOB 2 moves it to another step, KNOB 3 changes the parameter and KNOB 4 the value; past its lowest it's DELETE, and OCT+ does it. The top row turns them all on or off (PLAY ON) and clears them. All six tracks share 64 records.

  13. SEQ tools

    Tap HOME and hold SEQ: the SEQ TOOLS on the white keys, on any page. F3 CLEAR, G3 REVERSE, A3 and B3 SHIFT the steps left or right, C4 RANDOM, D4 COOK (a few notes changed), F4 EUCLID, D5 DOUBLE and E5 HALF the length with its steps; on a drum track E4 BEAT (a basic beat) and the lane tools on G4 to C5. The knobs are the PATTERN page's. Here REVERSE, then SHIFT >. Each tool is one undo (SAVE held, once SEQ is let go); OCT− puts back the track as it was when the layer opened.

  14. Euclid

    Hold SEQ and tap F4: EUCLID spreads HITS as evenly as they go over STEPS, turned by ROTATE, and plays it at once. A hit on a note keeps it, one on an empty step plays the scale's root. KNOB 1 HITS 5, KNOB 3 ROTATE 2, and OCT+ applies it (OCT− cancels). On a drum track it fills the selected lane.

09

SHAPE SOUND

Envelopes, LFOs, MOD

You'll learn to shape a sound over time with two envelopes, the LFO and the four mod slots, and to move knobs for a moment only.

  1. The envelope

    The beat from chapter 3 plays, and track 1 is selected: ACID, on ANALOG. Tap ENV for its envelope: ATTACK, DECAY, SUSTAIN and RELEASE, each time from 1 ms to 10 s, SUSTAIN a level. The graph draws its shape. KNOB 2 stretches ACID's DECAY from 54 to 160 ms. FM6 and the drum engines have no ENV pages (NO ENV ON FM6): they shape their notes on their own pages.

  2. Where it goes

    Tap ENV again for ENV DEST: how much of the envelope goes to the filter's CUTOFF, to the PITCH and to the SHAPE, each −100 to +100 %. ACID's squelch is CUTOFF +76 %: KNOB 1 down to +13 % dulls the bass, all the way up to +100 % makes every note bite. SHAPE moves the engine's shape: on ANALOG the pulse width of PWM, the second oscillator of SYNC.

  3. In time

    KNOB 4, SYNC ON: ATTACK, DECAY and RELEASE become note values, from 1/64T to 4BAR, and follow the tempo. Turn SELECT back to ENV to see them: DECAY reads 1/8D, and KNOB 2 takes it to 1/16, a note value a detent. SUSTAIN stays a level.

  4. ENV 2

    SELECT on to ENV 2: a second envelope with the same four knobs, always in milliseconds. On its own it changes nothing you hear: it's a source for the MOD slots, ENV2 (below). Here its DECAY goes up to 112 ms.

  5. The LFO

    Tap LFO: RATE (0.05 to 40 Hz), WAVE (SIN, TRI, SAW, SQR or S&H, a new random value each cycle), PHASE (where a note starts it) and FADE (how long it takes to come in). The graph draws two seconds of it. Here RATE 2.21 Hz and WAVE TRI; nothing moves yet, as every depth is still 0.

  6. LFO dest

    SELECT on to LFO DEST, the depths: PITCH, CUTOFF, SHAPE and AMP (a tremolo), each drawn as the wave it makes. KNOB 2, CUTOFF +50 %: the filter wobbles with the triangle. A little PITCH is a vibrato.

  7. LFO 2

    SELECT back to LFO 2. KNOB 1, SYNC, makes the LFO's cycle a note value of the tempo, 4BAR to 1/32; at 1/8 the wobble locks to the beat, and RATE dims. TRIG NOTE starts the LFO again at each new phrase, FREE lets it run. KNOB 3, POLARITY UNI, makes it one-sided: the filter only opens.

  8. MOD

    SELECT on to MOD: four slots, each a SOURCE sent to a DEST by an AMOUNT. The sources are the LFO, ENV, VEL, KEY, RAND (a new value each note), MODW and AT (the mod wheel and aftertouch over MIDI), EXPR, ENV2, S&H and SLEW; the destinations the pitch, the cutoff, the shape, the level, the pan, DIST and the sends, the LFO's RATE and DEPTH, the vibrato, the engine's own eight controls, and VEL and TIME. Slot 1 here: RAND to CUT, +63 %, so every note has its own brightness.

  9. Slot 2

    KNOB 1 picks the slot: 2. SOURCE ENV2, DEST PITCH, AMOUNT +25 %: each note starts three semitones sharp and drops into place as ENV 2 decays. The graph lists the four slots, the one being edited lit.

  10. Momentary

    Tap HOME. Hold LFO and turn knobs: the header says MOMENTARY, then OCT+: KEEP. Here KNOB 1 opens the CUTOFF and KNOB 2 adds RES; let go of LFO and they spring back at once: BACK. It works on HOME, the sound and FX pages and the MIXER, up to eight values at a time, and it's never recorded.

  11. Keep it

    Hold LFO again and turn KNOB 1, then tap OCT+ before you let go: KEPT, and the new values stay. A tap of LFO on its own still opens its pages.

SHAPE SOUND

Inside the engines

You'll learn each engine's own pages, the voice settings, drum lanes and slices.

  1. EDIT 1

    The beat from chapter 3 plays, with two chords on track 2. Tap EDIT on track 1: the engine's first page, named by the engine (ANALOG's is OSC). Every engine has up to eight controls of its own on EDIT 1 and EDIT 2. OSC is WAVE, DETUNE, MIX and NOISE, and the graph draws the wave: KNOB 3, MIX, brings in the second oscillator and KNOB 2 detunes it, SAW, A SECOND ONE 16 CT UP.

  2. EDIT 2

    Tap EDIT again for EDIT 2, FLT: CUTOFF, RES, DRIVE and KEYTRK, the low-pass drawn with its peak. KNOB 3, DRIVE, roughens the bass. Other engines fill these pages their own way: FM6 its algorithm, WHEEL its drawbars, SAMPLE the wave of the note played last; most draw the sound as it plays.

  3. Voice

    EDIT again: VOICE, how the track plays its notes. VOICE is POLY (a voice a note), MONO (one voice, a new attack each note), LEG (one voice, no new attack) or UNI (every voice on one note). ACID is LEG; KNOB 2, GLIDE, slides each note into the next, here 90 ms. GL MODE RATE keeps the speed the same, TIME the time; PRIORITY picks the note a MONO track plays when several are held.

  4. Voice 2

    ALGORITHM to track 2, the PAD, on its own VOICE page, POLY; SELECT on to VOICE 2: ALLOC (ROT takes the next voice in turn, so release tails ring out; REUSE the free voice nearest in pitch), DETUNE (UNI only) and SPREAD. KNOB 3, SPREAD, sends the chord's voices left and right of the track's PAN in turn. The boxes are the voices, lit as they sound.

  5. Another engine

    Tap EDIT: past the last page it goes round to EDIT 1, here FM6's, OPS: ALGO, FEEDBACK, MOD LVL and RATIO, the algorithm drawn as its operators. KNOB 3, MOD LVL, brightens the pad. FM6's own OPERATOR and ALL OPS pages come before VOICE: chapter 11.

  6. DRUM's kits

    ALGORITHM to track 4: DRUM's EDIT 1 is KIT, PITCH, TONE and DECAY. KNOB 1, KIT, swaps the whole kit: STD, the five virtual-analog kits 80, 10, 66, 55 and 77, and DEEP, TIGHT and PERC. KIT 80: a deep sine kick, a noisy snare.

  7. Lanes

    EDIT twice, past EDIT 2 (HIT), for LANES: each drum of the kit on its own. KNOB 1 picks the lane, KICK here; KNOB 2 is its LEVEL, KNOB 3 its TUNE (−6 semitones) and KNOB 4 its DECAY, longer (0 % is as the kit has it).

  8. DRUM-X

    Hold EDIT and tap E5: track 4 on DRUM-X (chapter 4), the same notes on its kit. DRUM-X synthesizes each of its eight sounds from an oscillator, a pitch envelope and noise, in two patches, A and B, with MORPH between them. One kit is the project's; LANES is DRUM's, so EDIT 1 opens instead (NO LANES ON DRUM-X).

  9. The SOUND sheet

    EDIT twice for SOUND: one sound of the kit on one sheet, BD first. PRESETS (or a key struck) picks the sound, SELECT moves the knobs down the sheet: PITCH, P MOD, DECAY, NOISE and COLOR, each A beside B, then LEVEL, DRIVE and the switches WAVE, FILTER, SNAP and P SHAPE. KNOB 1, PITCH A, takes the kick down.

  10. Slice

    ALGORITHM to track 6, hold EDIT and tap C5: SLICE, a drum break cut into slices (CHOP: BREAK in 16). Every key plays one, from F3, black keys too: F3 to C4 in order are its first half bar, and any order chops it up. Sequenced, the steps' notes pick the slices.

  11. The slices

    EDIT twice, past PLAYBACK (MODE, RVRS, DECAY, CUTOFF), for SLICES: the wave around a slice, its markers, and the whole sample under it. KNOB 1 steps through the slices, and a key shows the slice it plays. On your own samples KNOB 2 moves a marker and KNOB 3 and 4 split and join slices (OCT+ does it); the built-in loops are view only.

  12. Sources

    SELECT back to EDIT 1, SLICE: KNOB 1, SOURCE, runs BREAK, USR1, USR2, USR3, PIANO and FUNK; DIV cuts it in 4 to 32 or where the hits are (AUTO). FUNK is the second loop, two beats of funk. USR1–3 are your own samples, put on the unit with the web editor's Samples tab (chapter 19); SAMPLE and GRAIN play them too. An empty one plays a sine and says NO SAMPLE.

Glossary

Track
One of six parts, T1–T6, each with its own sound and pattern, all playing together. ALGORITHM picks the track you're working on.
Engine
How a track makes its sound. There are 14: ANALOG, FM6, PHASE, LOFI, SAMPLE, VOICE, TRIO, WHEEL, GRAIN, PHYS, NOISE, SLICE, DRUM and DRUM-X. Hold EDIT to switch.
Sound
A stored setting of an engine (a preset). PRESETS steps through them; your own go to SAVE › USER.
Page family
The pages one button opens: tap it again, or turn SELECT, for the next. SEQ's family is STEP, ROLL, CHANCE, PLAY, PATTERN, AUTOMATION and SONG.
Layer
What a held button lays over the page: FX, GLO, SEL, EDIT, SEQ TOOLS, PERFORM (ARP) and REC (SHIFT + REC). Let go and it's gone; SHIFT + the button keeps it open.
SHIFT
HOME held. Its map shows what every control does on the page, and it changes what some do: SHIFT + SAVE is TAKE.
Step
A sixteenth of a bar in a pattern, up to 64 of them. On the step pages the 16 white keys are steps 1–16.
Pattern
A track's notes exactly as they play: its steps, LENGTH, DIV, SWING, GATE, direction, automation and locks. Kept by itself; 64 of them in a pool every project shares.
List and entry
Each track has a list of 16 entries, A–P. An entry names a pattern and how many times it plays (REPS). The lists are the song, and PERFORM's keys.
Lock
A step's own value for a control: hold the step and turn a knob.
Take
SHIFT + SAVE keeps the loop you just played as the track's pattern, whether or not REC was on.
Scene
The entry every track is on and every mute, stored together (16 in a project) and recalled with one key in PERFORM.
Project
The sounds, the lists, the scenes and the settings of a piece of music. There are four; the patterns themselves live in the pool.
Undo
Hold SAVE. Eight changes are kept; OCT− goes further back and OCT+ redoes while the box shows.

If the FM-1 does not start

If an update is interrupted and the FM-1 stays black, check whether a computer sees it as a USB device named WL80UBOOT (or a USB mass-storage device with ID 4C4A:8057). That is the chip's built-in boot mode, and the FM-1 can be brought back:

  • First try another USB data cable and close every other app that uses MIDI, then run the installer again: it finishes an interrupted update.
  • If it stays in boot mode, FM-1 Transporter reads and writes the FM-1's flash from a Mac through a Seeed XIAO RP2040 (three wires to the FM-1's USB lines). Back up the flash first, then write the official firmware.

Anything else: open an issue on GitHub.
